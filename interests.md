I am interested in **low surface brightness (LSB) galaxies**, their detection and characterization
in large survey datasets like the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST). Most of my current work is focused on data from the Vera C. Rubin Observatory which is conducting the LSST, including its post-LSST capabilities. Some of my ongoing projects are in the **multi-messenger domain** with focus on the development of follow-up strategies for faint optical counterparts of binary neutron star mergers. I work on projections for the era of the third generation of gravitational wave detectors such as Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer in the late 2030s-2040s. I am also interested in the characterization of **transient host galaxies** (including GRB hosts) using Rubin/LSST and what the global picture of progenitors look like on the faint end of the galaxy mass function.

I am a member of the Rubin Galaxies Science Collaboration, the Rubin Transients and Variable Science (TVS) collaboration and the Einstein Telescope Collaboration. During my current employment as an assistant professor (adiunkt) at the NCBJ Warsaw, I am being funded by the NCN MAESTRO grant "Barely visible: Low Surface Brightness Universe in the LSST era" (2023/50/A/ST9/00579, PI: prof. dr. hab Agnieszka Pollo).
